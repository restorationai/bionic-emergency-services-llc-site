## What Should a Commercial Water Damage Contractor in Houston Be Able to Prove?

A commercial-ready contractor can show four things before a technician ever opens a drywall panel: trained and certified technicians, true around-the-clock response, active licensing and insurance, and a history of jobs bigger than a single bathroom or bedroom.

BIONIC's technicians hold IICRC Water Damage Restoration (WRT) and Applied Structural Drying (ASD) certifications, which means they've trained specifically on how water moves through different building materials and how to dry a structure according to the drying science in the [ANSI/IICRC S500 standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/). BIONIC has been responding to Houston-area water losses since 2011, is insured, and answers calls 24/7, which matters when a line breaks at 2 a.m. in a building with a restaurant on the ground floor and residential units above it.

Ask any contractor you're considering for proof of technician certifications, proof of insurance, and a sample of a commercial scope they've written. If they can't produce a sample scope, they haven't done many of these jobs.

## What Makes Commercial-Grade Drying Different From a Residential Job?

Commercial drying is a different scale of math than a residential bedroom, not just a bigger version of the same job. A 15,000 square foot office suite or a multi-floor retail building needs more air movers per square foot, larger low-grain-refrigerant or desiccant dehumidifiers, and daily psychrometric readings logged across every affected zone, not just one or two rooms.

That daily log matters for a reason beyond drying science. Commercial adjusters and property managers both want to see moisture readings trending down day over day, with dates and locations attached, because that log is what justifies how many drying days actually got billed. A contractor who shows up, sets a few air movers, and checks back in a week without documentation is not running a commercial-grade drying plan.

For buildings with multiple floors, mixed occupancy, or a loss that started on an upper level and traveled down through ceilings and electrical, the project needs a large loss response structure, not a standard residential crew size. BIONIC's [large loss response](/services/large-loss-response/) team scales staffing and equipment to match the square footage and number of affected floors rather than running a one-size crew on a multi-tenant loss.

## How Should a Contractor Handle the Insurance Adjuster on a Commercial Claim?

A commercial contractor worth hiring treats the adjuster as a partner in the file, not an obstacle, and documents the loss the same way whether or not an adjuster has shown up yet. That means photos and moisture readings from the first visit, a written scope before major work begins, and a single point of contact who can answer the adjuster's questions without the property manager relaying messages back and forth.

Commercial policies and coverage questions can get complicated fast, especially when business interruption, tenant improvements, or a shared building system is involved. The [Texas Department of Insurance](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/) is the state resource for understanding how a commercial property policy is supposed to respond to a covered loss, and it's worth a property manager's time to understand the basics before a claim gets filed, not after.

BIONIC's team documents every commercial job with daily logs and photos specifically so that record exists whether the loss is a small, uncomplicated repair or a disputed claim headed for appraisal.

## Why Does Mitigation Plus Reconstruction Under One Contract Matter for a Business?

Keeping mitigation and reconstruction under one contractor means one point of accountability from the moment water is extracted to the day tenants move back in, instead of re-bidding the rebuild to a second contractor after drying is done. For a business trying to reopen, that single handoff point usually means fewer delays and fewer gaps in communication.

A warehouse, office, or restaurant that sits half-dried for two weeks while the property manager interviews rebuild contractors is losing revenue the whole time.
