**TL;DR:** Storm damage restoration in the Houston area typically costs $1,500 to $4,000 for a single wet room with no rebuild, and $15,000 to $35,000 or more when wind-driven water hits multiple rooms and requires structural drying plus a partial rebuild. Large-loss hurricane damage can run past $100,000. The real number depends on how contaminated the water is, how many square feet are affected, how many days of drying equipment you need, and how much drywall, flooring, or roofing has to come out. A written scope from a crew that actually inspects the damage is the only number worth budgeting around.

If a thunderstorm just pushed rain under a few shingles in The Heights, or a tropical system drove water past a window seal near Buffalo Bayou Park, you're probably standing in front of a wet ceiling trying to figure out what this is going to cost before you even dial your insurance company. The honest answer is that storm damage restoration pricing swings wider than a standard pipe leak because storms bring wind, water, and sometimes contamination all at once. Here's what actually moves the number in Houston.

## How much does storm damage restoration cost in Houston?

Most Houston-area storm jobs fall somewhere between $1,500 for a minor, contained leak and $35,000 or more for a multi-room event with structural drying and rebuild. Here's how that typically breaks down by scenario.

| Scenario | Typical Cost Range |
|---|---|
| Single room, roof leak, Category 1-2 water, no rebuild needed | $1,500 - $4,000 |
| Multiple rooms, wind-driven rain intrusion, Category 3 water, contents affected | $4,000 - $9,000 |
| Moderate wind or hail damage with structural drying and partial rebuild (drywall, flooring) | $8,000 - $18,000 |
| Whole-home water intrusion, structural drying, contents pack-out | $15,000 - $35,000 |
| Large-loss, hurricane-level damage with extensive rebuild | $35,000 - $100,000+ |

These are typical industry ranges, not a quote. A job that looks like a single-room leak on day one can turn into a $9,000 job once a crew pulls back wet insulation and finds the water traveled into a second room. That's why a real inspection, not a phone estimate, is step one.

For context on how these numbers compare to non-storm water losses, our [water damage restoration cost guide](/blog/water-damage-restoration-cost-houston/) and [water mitigation cost breakdown](/blog/water-mitigation-cost-houston-tx/) cover the drying-only side of the equation in more detail.

## What drives the price up or down?

Five factors decide where a storm job lands on that table: water category, square footage, drying days, contents, and rebuild scope.

**Water category.** A straightforward rain leak through a roof usually starts as Category 1 (clean) water under the [IICRC S500 standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/), but it degrades fast once it sits against drywall and insulation. Wind-driven rain during a named storm or hurricane, and any surface water that floods in from outside, is treated as Category 3 (black water) from the start. Category 3 jobs cost more because more material gets removed instead of dried in place, and the crew works in full PPE with antimicrobial treatment on affected surfaces.

**Square footage affected.** A single bedroom costs less to dry and rebuild than a living room, hallway, and adjoining bedroom that all took water from the same roof failure.

**Drying days.** Air movers and dehumidifiers run on a daily rental and power cost. A Category 1 loss in a well-ventilated room might dry in 3 days. A Category 3 loss in a humid Houston summer with saturated framing can run 5 to 7 days or longer.

**Contents.** Furniture, flooring, and belongings that can be cleaned and dried cost less than items that need replacement. A pack-out and inventory for insurance adds cost but protects the claim.

**Rebuild scope.** Drywall, insulation, baseboards, flooring, paint, and roof repair are priced separately from the drying and extraction work, and they're usually the biggest line item on a large loss.

## Does homeowners insurance cover storm damage in Houston?

Most standard homeowners policies cover wind and hail damage as a sudden, accidental peril, including wind-driven rain that gets in through storm-caused roof or siding damage. What those same policies almost never cover is flood: rising or standing water, storm surge, or overflow from a bayou or creek. That requires a separate flood policy through the [National Flood Insurance Program](https://www.floodsmart.gov/get-insured/buy-a-policy) or a private flood insurer.

This split matters a lot in Houston, where neighborhoods like Meyerland sit close to bayou drainage and have flooded independently of wind events in past storms. If your home took on water because a storm ripped off shingles and rain came through the hole, that's typically a wind claim. If water rose up from outside and came in at the foundation, that's a flood claim, and without a flood policy, it's an out-of-pocket repair.

Two things protect a claim either way: documenting the damage with photos before cleanup starts, and beginning mitigation promptly, since most policies hold the homeowner to a duty to mitigate further loss. The [Texas Department of Insurance](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/consumer/disasters.html) publishes guidance on filing after a storm, and our [storm damage insurance claim checklist](/blog/storm-damage-insurance-claim-checklist/) walks through what to document before you call your adjuster.

## What happens during a storm damage restoration job?

A storm job moves through five phases, and understanding them is most of what explains the price.

1. **Emergency tarping and extraction.** The crew stops ongoing intrusion first (tarping a roof, boarding a broken window) and extracts standing water.
2. **Inspection and classification.** The technician determines the water category and class, maps the affected square footage, and documents everything with photos and moisture readings for the insurance file.
3. **Structural drying.** Air movers and dehumidifiers run for several days, with moisture levels checked daily until materials read dry.
4. **Contents evaluation.** Furniture and belongings are sorted into restore, clean, or replace, with a pack-out for anything that needs to leave the house.
5. **Rebuild.** Drywall, flooring, trim, paint, and any roof repair bring the space back to pre-loss condition.

A single-room Category 1 loss might only need phases 1 through 3. A hurricane-level loss touches all five, which is where the cost gap between $4,000 and $35,000 comes from.
