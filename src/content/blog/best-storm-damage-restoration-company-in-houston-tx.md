## What actually separates a good storm damage restoration company from a bad one in Houston?

Four things matter more than anything on a website: 24/7 availability, documentation quality, local knowledge of how Houston storms move water through a house, and whether mitigation and the insurance paperwork come from the same team.

Anyone can show up with a wet vac after a storm. What's harder to fake is a company that logs moisture readings room by room, knows why a slab home in Quail Valley dries differently than a two-story in The Woodlands, and has already dealt with Harris County insurers enough times to know what they'll ask for. Run any company you're considering through the sections below before you let them start work.

## Will the crew document the damage well enough for your insurance claim?

A company that's actually prepared for this will photograph every affected room, log moisture readings over the drying period, and hand you a written scope before major work starts, not after.

Drying follows the [ANSI/IICRC S500 standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/), which spells out how water categories and drying classes get assessed and tracked. That paperwork is what your adjuster uses to approve the claim, so a crew that skips it is leaving you to argue your own case later. BIONIC's technicians hold IICRC Water Damage Restoration (WRT) and Applied Structural Drying (ASD) certifications, and documentation is built into how the job gets scoped from the first walkthrough. Our [storm damage restoration insurance claim checklist](/blog/storm-damage-insurance-claim-checklist/) covers exactly what to collect before the adjuster calls.

It's also worth knowing what your policy actually covers before the storm hits. Most homeowners policies pay for sudden wind and rain damage, like a tree limb cracking a window or wind-driven rain forcing its way through a damaged roof. They generally exclude flood water, meaning storm surge, overflowing bayous, or rising groundwater, which requires a separate flood policy through the [National Flood Insurance Program](https://www.floodsmart.gov/) or a private flood carrier. The [Texas Department of Insurance](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/) has more on how that split typically plays out for Houston homeowners.

For most storm claims, yes: a local crew already knows which streets flood first and how long drying takes in Gulf Coast humidity, which shapes both the work and your timeline.

A company based here, with a real address and a history of jobs in Pasadena, Pearland, Sugar Land, and the neighborhoods around Buffalo Bayou, has a reason to still be answering the phone six months from now if something resurfaces. ## What should you ask before you sign a work authorization?

Ask these four questions before any crew starts pulling wet materials: are you available 24/7, who logs the documentation for my insurer, will the same company be handling the drying from start to finish, and are you local to Houston or dispatched in for this storm.

A company with a straight answer to all four, backed by real technician certifications and a verifiable local address, is the one worth letting into your house. If the answers get vague on any of those, especially the documentation question, that's your signal to keep calling.
