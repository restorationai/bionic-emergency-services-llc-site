## Should you call a plumber, the fire department, or a restoration company first?

Call a restoration company the moment standing water is touching flooring, drywall, baseboards, or belongings, even if the source has already been stopped. A restoration crew handles extraction, drying, and the documentation your insurer will want to see, which a plumber generally doesn't do.

Call a plumber instead, or shut off the main water valve yourself, only if water is still actively flowing and nobody has stopped it yet. Most Houston homes have a shutoff valve near the street-side water meter or under the kitchen sink. If you can't locate it or stop the flow, that's the one scenario where the plumber call comes before the restoration call.

Call 911 or your electric utility first, before anyone else, if there's standing water anywhere near an outlet, a breaker panel, or a window AC unit, or if you smell gas. [OSHA's flood response guidance](https://www.osha.gov/flood) flags electrical hazards as one of the most common causes of injury during water cleanup. Cut power at the breaker if you can do it safely from dry ground; otherwise wait for the utility or fire department to clear the space before anyone goes back in.

## What happens when you call a water damage restoration company?

The first call is a short triage conversation, not a sales pitch. Expect to be asked for your address, the water source if known (supply line, appliance, roof, backup), how long it's been sitting, and whether the power is on in the affected area.

From there, a technician is dispatched with extraction and drying equipment. On arrival, the crew checks moisture levels in walls and flooring with a meter, photographs the damage for your claim file, and starts pulling standing water with truck-mounted extractors. Drying equipment, air movers and dehumidifiers, gets placed and run to the drying targets described in the [ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage restoration standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/), the industry protocol for how fast and how dry a structure needs to get before equipment comes down.

If the water came from a toilet overflow with feces, a sewer backup, or floodwater from outside, say so on that first call. That's Category 3 water under the S500 standard, and it changes what gets removed versus dried, and what PPE the crew wears.

## Should you call your insurance company before or after the restoration crew?

Call the restoration crew first, then loop in your insurer once mitigation is underway. Most homeowners policies require you to limit further damage, and starting extraction promptly, with photos and a written scope, is exactly how you meet that requirement.

Sudden, accidental water damage from inside the home, a burst supply line or a failed appliance hose, is typically covered under a standard policy. Gradual leaks, long-term seepage, and flood from rising water outside the home usually aren't, and flood needs a separate NFIP or private flood policy. See the [Insurance Information Institute's guide to what's covered](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance) for the general pattern, and check your own policy language since coverage varies by insurer. Our own breakdown of [what homeowners insurance actually pays for water damage](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/) covers the specifics in more depth if you want to read it before you call anyone.

Keep photos of the standing water, the source if visible, and any damaged belongings before cleanup starts. A restoration company that documents the loss as part of the job, with a written scope and moisture logs, makes the claim conversation with your adjuster much shorter.
