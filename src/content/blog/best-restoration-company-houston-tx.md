## What makes a restoration company the right fit for a Houston home?

The right company carries current general liability insurance, employs technicians trained to the ANSI/[IICRC S500 water damage standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/), and can mobilize water extraction, fire mitigation, and storm tarping crews without waiting on a subcontractor three days out. That combination matters more in Houston than almost anywhere else, because a single storm season event (hurricane rain, a downed tree, a slab shift from clay soil) can produce two or three kinds of damage in the same house.

Specific things worth confirming before you hire anyone:

- Insured, with proof available on request
- Technicians holding IICRC Water Damage Restoration Technician (WRT) and Applied Structural Drying (ASD) credentials, not just general cleaning experience
- 24/7 live phone dispatch, not a voicemail queue
- Experience with Houston-specific risk, from bayou-adjacent flooding in neighborhoods like the Heights and Meyerland to slab leaks tied to the region's shifting clay soil
- A written scope of work, not a verbal estimate

## Restoration company vs. general contractor: what's the difference?

A restoration company specializes in emergency mitigation, meaning water extraction, structural drying, smoke and soot removal, and content pack-out, while a general contractor specializes in the rebuild once mitigation is finished. The two overlap at the handoff point: drywall, flooring, and paint.

Hiring the wrong one first costs time. A general contractor called to a fresh water loss may not own commercial dehumidifiers or know how to document moisture readings the way an insurer expects. A restoration company called in for a bathroom remodel with no active damage is the wrong tool for that job too. Some full-service restoration companies cross-train technicians across water, fire, and storm response so the same crew that stops the damage can also coordinate the drying equipment and insurance paperwork, start to finish, without a second vendor relationship.

## What should a restoration estimate actually include?

A real restoration estimate names the category and class of water involved, lists the specific drying equipment deployed and how many days it will run, and prices line items the way your insurer's own adjuster will, rather than quoting a flat lump sum. Vague estimates are the single biggest red flag in this industry.

At minimum, ask for:

1. The water category (clean, gray, or black) and what that means for which materials get removed versus dried in place
2. A day-by-day drying plan with equipment counts, not just "we'll monitor it"
3. Line-item pricing that matches standard restoration estimating software, since that's what most adjusters compare against
4. Clear language on who submits documentation to your insurer and when

If a company can't produce something like this in writing before work starts, that's worth pausing on.
