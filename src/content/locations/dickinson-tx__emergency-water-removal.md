**Water damage emergency in Dickinson?** Call now for emergency service. Dickinson sits low in the Galveston County coastal prairie, where Dickinson Bayou and the surrounding drainage network can turn a heavy rain event or a failed supply line into standing water across a slab foundation within hours. Whether the source is a burst pipe, a failed sump during a storm, or bayou overflow backing into a crawlspace, the clock on structural damage and microbial growth starts immediately, not the next morning.

## Why Dickinson Properties See Water Damage Issues

Most homes in and around Dickinson sit on slab-on-grade foundations built into flat, poorly draining coastal clay soil. That soil holds water instead of shedding it, so after a storm the ground around a foundation can stay saturated for days, pushing moisture up through slab penetrations and exterior walls long after the rain stops. Add Gulf Coast humidity that regularly sits above 70 percent for much of the year, and a wet wall cavity here doesn't get a chance to dry on its own the way it might in a drier climate. The region's flood history, including widespread residential flooding during Hurricane Harvey, means a lot of local housing stock has already been rebuilt once with water-resistant materials at the lower wall courses, which changes how we approach extraction and drying on a second event.

## Our Water Removal Process in Dickinson

We start with extraction, pulling standing water off hard flooring and out of carpet pad before it migrates further into subfloor and framing. In Dickinson specifically, we pay close attention to slab moisture readings, because clay soil saturation after a storm can keep wicking moisture into a slab for days even after surface water is gone. Once bulk water is out, we set containment and position air movers and dehumidifiers based on psychrometric readings rather than a standard room count, since humidity outside the structure directly affects how hard the equipment has to work inside it. We monitor moisture daily and document readings for the insurance file, drying to the IICRC S500 standard rather than guessing at a finish date.

## Dickinson Insurance & HOA Coordination

Many Dickinson homeowners carry flood coverage through the National Flood Insurance Program in addition to a standard homeowners policy, and the two typically cover different things: a slow seep from a plumbing failure is usually a homeowners claim, while bayou or surface water intrusion is often a flood policy matter. We document the water source, affected materials, and moisture readings in a way that supports whichever claim applies, and we can speak directly with your adjuster about what we found and why. If your property sits in an HOA-governed subdivision, we'll work around any exterior debris staging or dumpster placement rules so cleanup doesn't become its own headache on top of the water loss.

## Local note

A lot of Dickinson-area homes built or rebuilt after prior flood events used cement board or water-resistant drywall up to roughly the first two to four feet of wall height, a detail carried over from post-Harvey rebuilds. That changes our drying approach: those lower sections can often be dried in place rather than torn out, while older, unmodified sections of a home may still need standard drywall removed to dry the stud cavity properly. Knowing which material is behind the wall before we start cutting saves time and keeps repair costs down.
