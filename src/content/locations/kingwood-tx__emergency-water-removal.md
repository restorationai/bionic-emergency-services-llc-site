## Why Kingwood Properties See Water Damage

Most homes in Kingwood sit on concrete slab foundations, which is standard across the Houston area but creates a specific problem when a supply line or drain fails underneath the house: you don't see a puddle, you see a warm spot on the floor or a spike in the water bill. Add in the Gulf Coast's humid subtropical climate, where relative humidity routinely sits above 70% for weeks at a time, and the window before standing water causes secondary damage is shorter than homeowners expect. We also see a steady run of water calls tied to the San Jacinto River's West Fork, which runs along the community and has a documented history of overtopping during major storm events, including the flooding Kingwood experienced during Hurricane Harvey in 2017. Even away from the river, heavy regional rainfall can back up drainage around homes built in the 1970s through 1990s, when much of Kingwood's housing stock went in.

## Our Water Removal Process in Kingwood

Extraction starts with locating the source, whether that's a slab leak, a failed water heater, or storm intrusion, and shutting it off before anything else. From there we pull standing water with truck-mounted and portable extraction equipment, then move to subfloor and slab moisture mapping, because in a slab-foundation home the water that matters most is often the water you can't see. Drying is calibrated to our WRT and ASD training and the IICRC S500 standard, but the targets shift here: Gulf Coast ambient humidity means we often run dehumidification longer and at a lower target grain depression than a drier climate would require, especially in homes with original 1980s-era ductwork or crawlspace sections. We document moisture readings at each stage so there's a clear record of when the structure actually reached dry standard, not just when it looked dry.

## Kingwood Insurance Coordination

One of the more common questions we get locally is whether a standard homeowner's policy covers the loss, and the answer usually depends on the source. A burst pipe or appliance failure is typically a covered peril under most HO-3 policies, while water that enters from the West Fork or a rising lake level generally falls under NFIP flood coverage instead, which many Kingwood homeowners carry given the area's flood history. We photograph and log the water source, affected materials, and moisture readings at each visit so that whichever policy applies, the documentation supports the claim rather than leaving gaps for an adjuster to question later.

## Local note

A detail that catches a lot of Kingwood homeowners off guard: many homes built here between the late 1970s and mid-1990s were plumbed with polybutylene piping, a material that was popular across Houston-area suburbs during that building boom and is now known to become brittle and fail at fittings years later. If your home falls in that construction window and you've had one leak, it's worth having the rest of the system checked, because a single polybutylene failure is rarely an isolated event.
