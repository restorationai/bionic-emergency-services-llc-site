## Why La Porte Properties See Storm Damage

La Porte's exposure is less about any single storm and more about the combination of coastal wind, surge, and heat that follows. Homes here run from older frame construction near the bay to newer brick-and-slab builds further inland, and both styles take damage differently: older roofs with wind-worn decking lift and peel in gusts that a newer roof system would shed, while slab homes near low drainage points take on surge or rainfall faster than homes on higher ground. After the wind event passes, the Gulf Coast humidity that settles back in within hours accelerates damage to any wet drywall, insulation, or flooring that isn't dried quickly. Tree damage is also common on older residential lots with mature oak and pine cover, where a single downed limb can open a roof section to rain for days if it isn't tarped promptly.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in La Porte

We start with a structural walk to flag immediate safety hazards: compromised roof sections, exposed wiring, standing water near electrical panels. From there we stabilize the building envelope first, tarping roof openings and boarding broken windows or doors so wind-driven rain can't keep adding damage while claims and scheduling get sorted out. Once the structure is secure, we move into water extraction and drying, following IICRC-based drying standards to track moisture in framing, subfloor, and wall cavities rather than relying on a surface-dry look. Debris removal, including downed limbs and damaged fencing, typically runs alongside the drying phase so the property isn't sitting with hazards in the yard while interior work finishes. Throughout the job we document every affected area with photos and moisture readings, which becomes the backbone of the insurance file.

## La Porte Insurance Coordination

Storm claims in a coastal community like La Porte often split across two policies: wind and hail damage typically falls under a standard homeowners or windstorm policy, while storm surge or bay-driven flooding is usually a separate flood policy, often through NFIP. That split matters at claim time, because an adjuster assessing wind damage to a roof may not be the same adjuster handling water intrusion from surge, and the two causes of loss need to be documented separately even when they happened in the same storm. We photograph and log damage by cause where we can tell the difference, which helps keep the claim moving instead of stalling on which policy covers what.
