## Why Tomball Properties See Water Damage Issues

Tomball sits on expansive clay soil typical of the Houston metro's northwest corridor, and that soil swells and shrinks with seasonal rainfall in ways that stress slab foundations and the plumbing lines running through them. Combine that with the area's heavy spring and tropical-season rain events, and you get two common failure points: slab leaks that go undetected until flooring starts cupping, and surface water pooling against homes that don't have much natural grade to shed it. Add Gulf Coast humidity that regularly sits above 60% for much of the year, and any water intrusion that isn't fully dried out becomes a secondary damage risk within 24 to 48 hours rather than the week homeowners in drier climates might have to work with.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Tomball

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mount or portable units depending on how much access the property allows, then move into moisture mapping. On slab-foundation homes, that mapping matters more than it might elsewhere, because water trapped between the slab and finish flooring won't show up on a surface-level walkthrough. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to find where saturation is hiding under tile, laminate, or carpet pad, then set air movers and dehumidifiers calibrated to the room's size and the material's drying curve. Drying is tracked and documented against the IICRC S500 standard, which is also what most insurance adjusters expect to see before they'll sign off on a claim.

## Local note

Tomball homes built on slab foundations, which is most of the housing stock in this part of the county, trap moisture against the concrete when water gets in rather than draining down and away the way a home with a crawlspace might. That means the water you can see on the surface is often not the whole problem. We check moisture at the slab-to-wall juncture and behind baseboards first, since that's where saturation tends to sit longest after the visible puddle is gone, and it's the spot most DIY drying attempts miss entirely.
