## Why Conroe Properties See Fire Damage Issues

Conroe's growth has layered newer vinyl-and-engineered-wood construction in planned subdivisions over an older core of wood-frame homes built decades ago, and both building types react differently to fire and smoke. Older homes tend to have gaps around window casings and baseboards that let soot travel further into wall cavities, while newer homes with tighter building envelopes can trap smoke odor inside HVAC systems and attic insulation. Add in Montgomery County's long, humid summers, and soot residue that would stay dry and brushable in a drier climate instead reacts with moisture in the air to become acidic and corrosive within hours, etching into metal fixtures, appliances, and even glass if it isn't addressed quickly.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Conroe

We start with an assessment of the structure and a reading of what's salvageable versus what needs to come out, because fire damage decisions made in the first 24 to 48 hours affect whether flooring, cabinetry, and trim can be restored instead of replaced. Board-up and tarping come first if the fire has opened the roof or walls to the weather, which matters here given how quickly Gulf Coast humidity and summer storms can turn a compromised roofline into a secondary water problem. From there we extract firefighting water, remove soot with HEPA vacuums and chemical sponges suited to the surface, and run structural drying equipment calibrated to the kind of readings an IICRC ASD-trained technician looks for, not just surface-dry but dry through the stud and subfloor. Odor treatment, typically thermal fogging or hydroxyl generation depending on how deep the smoke traveled, happens last, once the structure itself is stable.

## Local note

Montgomery County's expansive clay soil is worth watching closely after any fire that involved significant firefighting water. Clay that absorbs that volume of water can swell and then shrink unevenly as it dries over the following weeks, sometimes showing up as new hairline cracks in slab foundations or drywall that weren't part of the original fire damage. We document pre-existing conditions and moisture readings at the foundation line specifically so that soil movement doesn't get mistaken for restoration-related damage later, which matters when an adjuster is reviewing the claim.
