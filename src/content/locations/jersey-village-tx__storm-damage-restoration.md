## Why Jersey Village Properties See Storm Damage

Much of Jersey Village was built out in the 1960s and 1970s, and the mature oak and pine canopy that gives the city its tree-lined character is also its biggest liability during a severe thunderstorm or tropical system. Older limbs shear off in straight-line winds and land on composition shingle roofs that were never engineered for direct impact loads. Add in a flat, low-lying stretch of Harris County where ditches and bayous drain slowly, and a roof breach during a heavy rain event turns into standing water in the attic or under flooring within hours, not days. Hurricane remnants that push up from the Gulf tend to stall over this part of the county, so homes here often see prolonged rain on top of wind damage rather than a quick-hitting storm and clear skies.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Jersey Village

The first call is about stopping the bleeding: tarping an open roof, boarding a blown-out window, and getting water extraction equipment on-site before it migrates into wall cavities or subflooring. From there we document the damage room by room with photos and moisture readings, which matters for the claim process and for deciding what materials can be dried in place versus what has to come out. Homes in this part of Houston frequently have slab foundations with carpet or engineered wood directly over the concrete, so our drying plan accounts for how quickly moisture wicks sideways under flooring that looks dry on the surface. Structural drying follows IICRC ASD protocols, and any standing water or storm-driven intrusion is extracted and monitored under IICRC WRT guidelines until moisture content in the affected materials returns to a stable baseline.
