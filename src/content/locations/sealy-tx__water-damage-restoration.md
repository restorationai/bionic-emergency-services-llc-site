## Why Sealy Properties See Water Damage Issues

Austin County sits in a humid subtropical pocket of Texas that cycles between dry stretches and sudden, heavy rain. That pattern is hard on foundations. Many Sealy properties, whether an older home near the downtown core or a newer build out along the I-10 corridor, sit on clay-rich soil that expands when saturated and contracts when it dries out. That movement stresses slab foundations and the plumbing lines running through or under them, which is why we see a steady pattern of slab leaks and supply line failures that aren't always obvious until a homeowner notices a warm spot on the floor or a spike in the water bill. Older housing stock closer to downtown also tends to carry original cast iron or galvanized plumbing, which corrodes from the inside and fails without much warning. Newer construction isn't immune either; it's more likely to see water intrusion from roof flashing or window penetrations during the kind of wind-driven rain that comes through with Gulf storm systems.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Sealy

We start with an assessment of the source and the category of water involved, because a slab leak behaves differently than storm intrusion through a roof. Extraction comes first, pulling standing water off hard flooring and out of saturated carpet pad before it wicks further into baseboards and framing. From there we map moisture with meters across drywall, subfloor, and structural members, not just the visibly wet areas, because water travels along framing and behind wall cavities in ways that aren't obvious from the surface. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with air movers and dehumidification sized to the actual cubic footage and material types in the space rather than a generic setup, and we monitor daily until moisture readings confirm the structure is back to a dry standard, not just dry to the touch.
