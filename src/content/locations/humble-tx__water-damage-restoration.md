## Why Humble Properties See Water Damage Issues

Humble sits low and close to Lake Houston and the San Jacinto River, which means heavy rain events don't just fall, they pool. Add in the gumbo clay soil common across this part of Harris County, which expands when wet and contracts when dry, and you get slab foundations that shift enough over the years to crack supply lines underneath the house. A lot of our calls here start the same way: a homeowner notices a warm spot on the floor, a water bill that doesn't make sense, or a faint mildew smell weeks before they ever see visible water. By the time it's visible, it's usually been traveling under the slab or along a wall cavity for a while.

The climate doesn't help. Humble's humid subtropical summers keep ambient moisture high even when it isn't actively raining, so a wet wall cavity that might dry on its own in a drier climate just sits and stays wet instead. We've also seen the aftermath of the bigger regional flood events, Harvey being the obvious one, where homes that took on water from rising creeks and bayous dealt with both the initial intrusion and a longer secondary drying problem because the whole neighborhood's humidity spiked at once.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Humble

Water extraction comes first, pulling standing water with truck-mounted and portable extraction units before it migrates further into subfloor and framing. From there we moisture-map the affected rooms with meters and sometimes thermal imaging, because in slab-foundation homes the visible wet spot is rarely the whole story, water moves along the path of least resistance under tile and engineered flooring long before it surfaces.

Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard: air movers and dehumidifiers are placed and adjusted based on daily moisture readings, not on a fixed calendar. Homes with original hardwood or older plaster-and-lath sections (still found in some of Humble's older housing stock near the historic downtown area) dry more slowly than newer drywall construction, so we recheck those areas more frequently rather than pulling equipment on a set schedule. Once materials read dry and stable, we treat affected surfaces for microbial growth before closing out the drying phase.

## Humble Insurance & HOA Coordination

Most of the claims we document in Humble fall under a homeowner's policy for sudden water damage (a burst pipe, a failed water heater, a supply line crack), which is handled differently than flood damage tied to rising water from the lake or a creek, the latter typically requiring separate flood coverage. We photograph and document moisture readings room by room so the distinction is clear on paper, because carriers will ask. If your property is in an HOA-governed subdivision, we're used to working around exterior-access rules and will coordinate equipment staging with property management when a unit or townhome requires it.

## Local note

One thing we watch for specifically in Humble: homes built in the slab-foundation boom years, mostly ranch-style construction from the 1970s and 80s, often have copper supply lines running directly under the slab with no accessible shutoff nearby. When one of these develops a pinhole leak, water can spread laterally under multiple rooms before it ever surfaces, so we treat a single visible wet spot in these homes as a reason to check the whole slab footprint, not just the room where the stain appeared.
