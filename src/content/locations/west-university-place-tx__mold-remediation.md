Mold remediation in West University Place almost always starts the same way: a homeowner notices a musty smell near a bathroom or utility closet weeks after a slow leak they thought they'd already fixed. Between the Gulf Coast humidity and the slab-on-grade construction common throughout this part of Harris County, moisture that gets trapped behind drywall or under cabinetry has very little chance to dry on its own, and colonization can begin within 24 to 48 hours of a sustained leak.

## Why West University Place Homes See Mold Issues

West University Place sits in a flat, low-lying stretch of the Houston metro where heavy clay soils hold water close to the surface long after a storm passes. That soil moisture pushes against slab foundations and crawlspace vapor barriers, and it's a major reason so many homes here deal with recurring dampness in first-floor walls and flooring. Add in central Texas summers where outdoor humidity regularly sits above 70 percent, and air conditioning systems that aren't sized or maintained correctly become a second moisture source: condensation lines back up, supply ducts sweat inside unconditioned attics, and that moisture settles into insulation and drywall paper, which is one of mold's preferred food sources. Many of the houses in this area were built mid-century with plumbing and roof systems now decades past their original service life, so slow leaks inside wall cavities often go unnoticed until the smell or visible staining shows up.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in West University Place

We start with a moisture map of the affected area using a combination of visual inspection and moisture meters, because surface mold is usually only part of the problem. Once we've identified the scope, we isolate the work zone with physical containment and negative air pressure so spores don't migrate into the rest of the home while work is underway, following the practices outlined in the IICRC S520 mold remediation standard. Affected porous materials like drywall, insulation, and in some cases subflooring are removed and bagged for disposal rather than treated in place, since porous materials that have been colonized generally can't be fully cleaned. Remaining structural surfaces are HEPA vacuumed and treated, and we run air scrubbers through the containment area until particle counts come back down to an acceptable range before we clear the space for reconstruction or reoccupancy.

## Local note

One detail that catches a lot of West University Place homeowners off guard: because many homes here have additions or renovated bathrooms built onto the original slab at a slightly different elevation, moisture can travel laterally along that seam and show up as a mold patch in a room that never had a leak of its own. We check those transition points specifically during inspection, since treating only the room with the visible stain can miss where the moisture is actually coming from.
