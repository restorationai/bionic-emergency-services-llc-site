## Why Tomball Properties See Water Damage Issues

Tomball sits where Harris and Montgomery counties meet, an area built mostly on expansive clay soil that swells when saturated and shrinks when dry. That movement stresses slab foundations and the plumbing running through them, which is why so many of the water calls we take here start with a slab leak rather than a visible pipe failure. Add in the region's heavy spring and hurricane-season downpours, and you get a second pattern: water pushing up through foundation joints or backing up through yard drains faster than it can be pumped out. Most of Tomball's housing stock was built from the 1970s forward as slab-on-grade construction, with a smaller cluster of older buildings near the historic downtown core that date back to the town's early rail days. Older structures tend to have older cast iron or galvanized supply lines, which fail differently than the PEX and copper common in newer subdivisions, and that affects where water travels once it escapes the pipe.

## Our Water Removal Process in Tomball

We start by identifying the water category, clean, gray, or black, since that determines how we handle extraction and what materials can be saved. Standing water comes out first with truck-mounted or portable extraction units, followed by moisture mapping of walls, subfloor, and slab to find water that's migrated beyond what's visible. On slab homes, which make up most of what we see in Tomball, water often travels under baseboards and into wall cavities before it ever shows as a stain, so we check moisture content at multiple points rather than relying on a visual inspection alone. Drying equipment, air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the affected square footage, stays in place until materials read dry per the IICRC S500 standard, not just until the floor looks dry to the eye. Carpet pad is almost always a total loss in a Category 2 or 3 event; carpet itself can sometimes be salvaged depending on dwell time.

## Local note

One thing that surprises a lot of Tomball homeowners: a slab leak that shows up as a warm or damp spot on the flooring can have been running for days or weeks before anyone notices, because the clay soil under the slab absorbs a lot of water before it reaches the surface. By the time carpet or tile grout starts showing moisture, the subfloor and insulation below the slab may already be saturated, which is part of why we always check moisture levels well beyond the visible wet area rather than treating only what's stained.
