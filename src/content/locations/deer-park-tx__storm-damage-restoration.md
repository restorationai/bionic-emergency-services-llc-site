## Why Deer Park Properties See Storm Damage

Deer Park sits low and flat along the Ship Channel corridor, and the gumbo clay soil underneath doesn't drain the way sandier soil does further inland. When a tropical system or a summer microburst dumps a few inches in an hour, water tends to pond in yards and along curb lines instead of soaking in, which means it finds its way into garages, crawlspaces, and slab penetrations even on homes that came through the wind fine. A lot of the city's housing stock dates to the decades after World War II, when the neighborhood grew up around the petrochemical plants, and those original ranch-style roofs and fascia boards are now old enough that wind uplift and hail routinely expose decades-old decking that wasn't built to current wind-load expectations. Add mature pine and oak canopy in the older sections of town, and tree limb strikes on roofs are one of the most common calls we get after a front moves through.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Deer Park

The first visit is about stopping the damage from getting worse: tarping open roof sections, boarding broken windows, and extracting any standing water before it works into subflooring or drywall. From there we moisture-map the affected areas and document everything for the claim before any materials come out, because Harris County storm claims move faster when the carrier gets clear photos and readings up front rather than a verbal description after the fact. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, and our techs hold IICRC WRT and ASD training, so the drying plan accounts for how slowly Gulf Coast humidity lets a building release moisture compared to a drier climate. Once materials are stable and dry, we coordinate with your contractor or our reconstruction partners on anything that needs rebuilding, from roof decking to drywall and trim.
