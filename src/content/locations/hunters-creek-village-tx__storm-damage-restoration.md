## Why Hunters Creek Village Properties See Storm Damage Issues

Homes in this part of the Memorial Villages tend to sit on large, heavily wooded lots, and that tree canopy is both the neighborhood's biggest asset and its biggest liability during a storm. Mature oaks drop limbs under straight-line wind gusts, and root systems that have lifted over decades can cause a tree to fail even when the trunk looks healthy. Add in the flat terrain and dense clay soil typical of this stretch of Harris County, and you get slow-draining yards that funnel water toward foundations and crawlspaces long after the storm cell has moved on. Many houses here date to the postwar ranch-style building boom of the 1950s and 1960s, with roof decking and flashing details that weren't engineered for the wind speeds we now see regularly, so wind-driven rain finds its way behind siding and into attic insulation more easily than it would in newer construction.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Hunters Creek Village

The first visit is about stopping the damage from spreading, not just cleaning up what already happened. That means emergency tarping or board-up on compromised roof sections, extraction of standing water from low-lying yards and garages, and moisture mapping of walls and attic spaces that took on wind-driven rain. Our technicians, trained to IICRC WRT and ASD standards, document every affected material with photos and moisture readings before any demolition starts, because that documentation is what carriers rely on to approve the claim. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 guidelines, with drying equipment placed and monitored daily until readings confirm the structure, not just the surface, is dry. Debris and downed limbs are cleared separately from structural repair so a homeowner isn't waiting on a tree service before interior drying can begin.
