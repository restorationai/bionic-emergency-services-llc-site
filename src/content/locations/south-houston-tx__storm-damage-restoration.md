## Why South Houston Properties See Storm Damage Issues

South Houston's flat, low-lying terrain drains slowly even on a calm day, and Sims Bayou is the primary outlet for stormwater across the area. When a tropical system or a heavy summer cell dumps several inches in an hour, the bayou backs up faster than storm drains can clear, and water finds its way into garages, slab foundations, and ground-level additions before a homeowner even realizes the roof has a problem too. Add the area's proximity to the Ship Channel industrial corridor, where wind gusts tend to funnel unobstructed across open lots and rail yards, and you get a pattern of roof and fence damage that's often more severe than what's reported a few miles north in central Houston. Much of the housing stock here dates to the postwar decades, with low-slope roof additions, older fascia and soffit systems, and slab foundations that weren't built with today's rainfall intensity in mind. That combination means wind-driven rain gets behind flashing and into attic insulation faster than it would in newer construction.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in South Houston

The first visit is about stopping the bleeding: tarping exposed roof sections, boarding broken windows, and extracting standing water before it wicks into drywall and subfloor. From there we run moisture readings on framing and slab assemblies, because a South Houston home that took on bayou backflow often reads dry at the surface while the slab edge and bottom plates stay saturated for days. Drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with air movers and dehumidification sized to the affected square footage, not a one-size-fits-all setup. Once the structure is stable, we document everything, torn shingles, saturated insulation, fence sections, downed limbs, with photos and moisture logs that match what an adjuster will need to see.

## South Houston Insurance Coordination

Storm claims in this part of Harris County tend to get complicated because wind damage and bayou-related flooding are often covered under separate policies or endorsements. A roof torn open by wind is typically a standard homeowner's claim, but water that backed up from Sims Bayou during the same event may fall under a flood policy instead, which changes the documentation an adjuster expects. We photograph and log damage in a way that separates wind-driven loss from floodwater intrusion from the start, so the claim doesn't get stuck in a coverage dispute while materials sit wet.
