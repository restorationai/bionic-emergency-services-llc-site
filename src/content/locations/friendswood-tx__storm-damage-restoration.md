Friendswood sits low along Clear Creek between Harris and Galveston counties, which means the same storm system that drops heavy rain on Houston often stalls here and keeps dumping water onto saturated clay soil long after the wind dies down. When a line of severe thunderstorms, a tropical system, or a spinoff tornado tears through, the damage isn't just the roof or the fence line, it's what happens underneath: water finding its way into slab foundations, insulation, and wall cavities before anyone notices.

## Why Friendswood Properties See Storm Damage Issues

Much of Friendswood's housing stock was built on slab foundations in a floodplain that drains slowly even on a good day. Add a fast-moving storm cell off the Gulf, and Clear Creek and its tributaries can back up faster than local drainage can clear it. Wind-driven rain gets pushed sideways into soffits and window flashing that were never built for sustained tropical gusts, and mature tree canopy throughout the city means downed limbs and uprooted trees are a routine part of storm cleanup here, not an exception. Homes near the creek corridor tend to see water intrusion at the slab edge first, while homes further from the water more often report roof and attic damage from wind and hail.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Friendswood

We start with a structural safety check before anyone steps inside, because downed power lines and compromised roof decking are common after Gulf Coast storms. From there, our crews document the loss with photos and moisture readings, tarp or board any open roof and wall penetrations to stop ongoing water entry, and extract standing water where applicable. Drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with our technicians trained to IICRC WRT and ASD protocols for structural moisture that's worked its way into wall cavities and subfloor, which is the part of storm damage that homeowners underestimate most often. Tree debris and downed limbs are cleared and staged for hauling, and we coordinate reconstruction scope with your adjuster once the structure is dry and documented.

## Friendswood Insurance & HOA Coordination

Most Friendswood neighborhoods fall under some form of deed-restricted community or HOA, and a number of those communities have exterior standards around fencing, roofing materials, and tarping that matter during storm recovery, not just during routine remodeling. We photograph damage thoroughly before any temporary repair goes up, which protects your claim and gives you something concrete if your HOA asks why a blue tarp is on the roof for a few weeks. On the insurance side, wind and hail claims in this part of the Gulf Coast often get scrutinized closely for pre-existing roof wear versus storm-caused damage, so our documentation is built with that in mind from the first visit.

## Local note

One thing that catches homeowners off guard here: because Friendswood sits where Harris and Galveston counties meet along Clear Creek, two storms that look identical on radar can produce very different damage depending on which side of the creek a property sits. Homes on the Galveston County side tend to see slower drainage and longer standing water after heavy rain events, which changes how long we leave drying equipment running, sometimes by several extra days, even when the visible damage looks the same as a house a few streets away.
