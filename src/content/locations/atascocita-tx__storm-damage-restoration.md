## Why Atascocita Properties See Storm Damage

Atascocita sits along the FM 1960 corridor northeast of Houston, close enough to Lake Houston and the San Jacinto watershed that drainage is a constant factor in how storms play out on individual lots. Heavy thunderstorms and the tail end of Gulf tropical systems drop a lot of rain fast, and the area's clay-heavy soil doesn't absorb it quickly. Water sits in roadside ditches and backyard swales longer than homeowners expect, which means wind damage to a roof or siding often gets compounded by standing water finding its way into the structure before anyone's noticed the leak.

The tree canopy adds another layer. Loblolly pines and mature oaks throughout the older sections of Atascocita are beautiful until a straight-line wind event or a tornado-warned cell snaps limbs or drops a whole tree onto a roofline. Pine limbs in particular tend to puncture rather than just crush shingles and decking, which opens a direct path for water intrusion that isn't always visible from the ground.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Atascocita

The first call is almost always about stopping active water intrusion, so our crews prioritize emergency tarping and board-up on compromised roof sections or broken windows before anything else. From there we move to tree and debris coordination, working around whatever a tree service or the homeowner's own crew is doing to clear limbs, so the structure can actually be assessed and dried.

Once the exterior is secured, we map moisture through the affected framing, insulation, and drywall. A lot of Atascocita homes built from the 1970s through the 2000s have asphalt shingle roofs over standard decking, which means wind-driven rain can travel along the underlayment and show up as a stain several feet from the actual breach point. We dry structures per the IICRC S500 standard, which guides how long equipment stays and when materials are actually dry enough to close back up, rather than just when they look dry on the surface.
