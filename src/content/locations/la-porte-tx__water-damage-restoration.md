## Why La Porte Properties See Water Damage Issues

La Porte's location on Galveston Bay puts it squarely in the path of Gulf moisture, tropical downpours, and the occasional storm surge that pushes water further inland than homeowners expect. Add Harris County's heavy clay soil, which expands when saturated and contracts when it dries, and you get foundation movement that cracks slab joints and opens new paths for water to migrate under flooring. Homes built in the decades when La Porte grew alongside the Houston Ship Channel's petrochemical and shipping industry often carry original plumbing runs that are now reaching the end of their service life, and failed polybutylene or galvanized supply lines are a recurring cause of the calls we get from this part of the bay area. High year-round humidity also means that even a modest leak behind a wall can sit undetected for weeks, since surface drywall can look and feel dry while the insulation and framing behind it stay wet.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in La Porte

We start with moisture mapping, using meters to find out how far water has traveled beyond the visible wet spot, because in La Porte's humid climate that spread is usually wider than it looks. Standing water gets extracted first, then we set up structural drying equipment calibrated to the ambient humidity, since Gulf Coast air carries more moisture than the air conditioner alone can offset. Our technicians hold IICRC Water Restoration (WRT) and Applied Structural Drying (ASD) certifications, and drying follows IICRC S500 monitoring intervals so we're not guessing when a wall cavity or subfloor has actually reached dry standard, we're measuring it. For homes near the bay with slab foundations, we pay particular attention to perimeter walls where clay soil movement has opened gaps that let groundwater wick upward after heavy rain.
