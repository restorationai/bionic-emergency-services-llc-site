## Why Fresno Properties Face Added Fire Damage Risk

Fresno sits in Fort Bend County along the Highway 6 corridor, where a mix of older single-family homes and newer construction means fire behaves differently depending on the block. Older homes with wood lath, asbestos-era insulation, or original 1970s-80s wiring tend to see more electrical-origin fires and smoke that travels through balloon-frame wall cavities. Newer subdivisions closer to Sienna and the Brazos River floodplain are built tighter, which can trap smoke odor in HVAC systems and attic insulation longer after the flames are out. Either way, the region's humid subtropical climate means any water used to extinguish a fire, whether from sprinklers or hose lines, starts creating secondary moisture problems within hours if it isn't addressed alongside the fire cleanup itself.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Fresno

We start with a walk-through to separate what's structurally sound from what isn't, since fire damage repair decisions hinge on whether framing, subfloor, or roof decking were compromised by heat or firefighting water. From there, we board up openings and tarp roof damage to stop weather intrusion, which matters in Fresno given how quickly afternoon thunderstorms roll in off the Gulf during spring and summer. Soot and smoke residue get cleaned using methods matched to the surface, dry sponging for porous materials, wet cleaning for washable surfaces, and HEPA vacuuming throughout to capture airborne particulate before it resettles. Structural drying follows IICRC ASD guidelines for any water introduced during suppression, and we document moisture readings throughout so the drying log supports the insurance file. Odor control is handled last, after the source materials are removed or cleaned, not masked over with fragrance while soot is still embedded in porous materials.

## Local note

One thing that catches homeowners off guard in Fresno: slab foundations are the norm here, which means fire suppression water doesn't drain away the way it might with a crawlspace or basement elsewhere. Water pools against the slab edge and under cabinetry, and if that's not extracted and dried properly within the first day or two, it creates a second problem layered on top of the fire damage, specifically swollen baseboards and delaminating cabinet bases that show up a week later. We check moisture at the slab line on every fire job here for that reason, not just at visible water lines.

Most fire losses in Fresno go through homeowners insurance, and carriers typically want documentation of both the fire damage and any water damage from suppression efforts tracked separately, since they're sometimes covered under different policy provisions.
