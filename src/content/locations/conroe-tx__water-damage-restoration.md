## Why Conroe Properties See Water Damage Issues

Conroe sits on expansive clay soil typical of this stretch of the Gulf Coastal Plain, and that soil moves. It swells when saturated and shrinks during dry spells, which stresses slab foundations and the plumbing running through them over time. Add in the humid subtropical climate and the frequent heavy downpours that roll off Lake Conroe and the San Jacinto River watershed, and you get a region where both slow plumbing leaks and sudden storm intrusion are common. Homes built before the 1990s around the older sections of town often still have original cast iron or galvanized supply lines, which corrode from the inside and fail without warning. Newer construction out toward the Grand Parkway corridor isn't immune either; PEX fittings and quick-build slab work can leave gaps that show up as water intrusion within the first few years.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Conroe

We start with moisture mapping, not guesswork. Our technicians use penetrating and non-penetrating meters to find out how far water traveled, because in a slab home that often means checking adjacent rooms that look dry on the surface but are wicking moisture through the drywall base. Extraction comes next, pulling standing water from flooring, carpet pad, and cabinetry before it sits long enough to start breaking down materials. Then we set up structural drying equipment calibrated for Conroe's ambient humidity, which runs higher than drier parts of the state for much of the year and can stall evaporation if drying isn't managed with dehumidification alongside air movement. We follow IICRC S500 drying standards throughout and document moisture readings daily so there's a clear record for your insurance file.
