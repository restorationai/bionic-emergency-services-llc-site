## Why Richmond Properties See Sewage Backups

Richmond sits low relative to the Brazos River, and much of the city's older housing stock was built on clay-heavy soil that expands when saturated and contracts when it dries out. That movement is hard on buried sewer lines, especially cast iron and clay pipe common in homes built before municipal upgrades modernized the system. Add in heavy regional rainfall events that overwhelm municipal lines or push groundwater into cracked laterals, and you get backups that show up as a sewer smell in a bathroom, gurgling drains, or standing wastewater in a garage or utility room. Properties on septic systems in the unincorporated parts of Fort Bend County face a related but distinct problem: saturated drain fields that can't absorb effluent fast enough during wet stretches, leading to surface pooling or backups into the house itself.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process

Sewage work isn't water damage work with extra steps, it's its own category of loss because of contamination risk. We start by identifying the water category, in most backup scenarios this is Category 3, grossly contaminated water that requires full removal of porous materials it has touched, not just drying. Carpet, pad, and often baseboards and drywall in the affected zone typically can't be saved once raw sewage has soaked in. After extraction and material removal, we clean and disinfect hard surfaces, then use negative air pressure and HEPA filtration to manage airborne contaminants while structural drying brings the space back to a safe moisture level. Our technicians carry IICRC credentials in water restoration and structural drying, which matters here because getting the structure dry correctly after a sewage event, not just clean, is what prevents secondary problems like wood rot or microbial growth later.
