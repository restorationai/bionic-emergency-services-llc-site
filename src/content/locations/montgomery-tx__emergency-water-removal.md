## Why Montgomery Properties See Water Damage Issues

Montgomery sits in a part of Southeast Texas where expansive clay soils shift with every wet-dry cycle, and that movement stresses slab foundations, plumbing joints, and the seams where pipes penetrate concrete. Add a humid subtropical climate with heavy spring and hurricane-season rainfall, and you get two common failure points: slab leaks that go unnoticed for weeks because the water migrates sideways under flooring before it surfaces, and surface flooding around properties that still rely on well and septic systems, common in Montgomery County's unincorporated areas outside the city core. Newer subdivisions built on cleared land near Lake Conroe can also see faster runoff and ponding during heavy rain, since the native soil's absorption rate hasn't caught up with the grading changes from recent development. Older rural homes, meanwhile, often have crawlspace foundations where a slow leak can sit undetected until the smell of damp wood gives it away.

## Our Water Extraction Process in Montgomery

The first step is always finding out how far the water traveled, not just where it's visible. We use moisture meters and infrared imaging to track water that's migrated under flooring or into wall cavities, since clay-soil slab leaks in particular tend to push moisture well past the obvious wet spot. Extraction comes next, pulling standing water with truck-mounted or portable units depending on access, followed by placement of air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the structure and the material involved. Drywall dries faster than the solid wood paneling and real plaster still found in some of Montgomery's older farmhouses and ranch-style homes, so we adjust drying timelines and check points by material rather than running a single generic schedule. Drying follows IICRC S500 standard practices, and our technicians hold IICRC WRT and ASD training, so moisture readings and drying logs are documented the way an insurance adjuster expects to see them.
