## Restoration emergencies common in Houston

Houston sits low and flat against a high water table, which means heavy rain doesn't just run off, it backs up. Bayou-adjacent streets from Meyerland to the Heights see street flooding and slow-draining yards that push water against foundations for hours after a storm passes. Add in a long, humid summer and a hurricane season that runs June through November, and the combination of standing water, saturated soil, and high ambient humidity creates ideal conditions for mold to start colonizing drywall and framing within 24 to 48 hours of a leak going unnoticed. Roof and siding damage from straight-line wind or hail is common after the stronger summer storm systems, and older housing stock throughout the city means plumbing failures, not just weather, account for a steady share of the calls we get.

## Services we provide in Houston

Water damage restoration is the call we get most, and in Houston it's rarely just "a wet carpet." Slab leaks, bayou-adjacent flooding, and storm intrusion often mean water has traveled further into subfloor and framing than it looks on the surface, which is why we extract, measure moisture, and dry structures per the IICRC S500 standard rather than just running a fan and calling it done.

Fire and smoke damage cleanup addresses not just visible char but the smoke residue that settles into drywall, HVAC systems, and fabric throughout a home, something that's especially persistent in the tightly built bungalows common in neighborhoods like the Heights.

Storm damage response covers wind, hail, and flood-driven losses, including emergency tarping and board-up to stop further water intrusion while repairs are scoped.

Mold remediation and inspection follows naturally from Houston's humidity and flood history, since any water event left untreated for more than a day or two can turn into a secondary mold problem.

Biohazard cleanup handles sewage backflow, which is a recurring issue in older sewer lines feeding neighborhoods like Third Ward and EaDo, along with other contamination that needs to be removed and disposed of safely.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Houston

Much of Houston's housing stock dates from the postwar decades through the 1980s, with slab-on-grade foundations dominating rather than basements or crawlspaces, which means water intrusion tends to wick up through drywall and baseboards rather than pool underneath a structure. Plumbing in that era-built housing ranges from galvanized and cast iron to early copper, and homes from the 1970s may still carry polybutylene supply lines, both of which are prone to age-related failure. The region's heavy clay soil expands and contracts with moisture swings, which stresses slab foundations and is part of why so many Houston water losses start as a slab leak rather than a visible pipe break. Harris County and the City of Houston both have a role in permitting structural repair work, and flood-zone designations near the bayous can affect what rebuild work requires additional review.
