## Restoration emergencies common in Seabrook

Seabrook sits directly on Galveston Bay, which means most restoration calls here trace back to water in one form or another. Gulf Coast humidity keeps indoor moisture levels high for much of the year, so a roof leak or plumbing failure that would dry out in a drier climate instead sits and causes secondary damage such as swelling, warping, and lingering odors. Hurricane season brings storm surge risk and wind-driven rain that finds its way through soffits, window flashing, and garage door seals on homes that were never designed for sustained saltwater exposure. Between storms, the bigger driver is simply age and moisture: older supply lines and slab plumbing in a coastal environment corrode and fail more often than they would further inland, and a slow leak under a slab can go unnoticed until flooring starts to cup or baseboards start to smell musty.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Seabrook

Much of Seabrook's housing stock dates from the 1960s through the 1990s, built slab-on-grade like most of the Texas Gulf Coast, with brick veneer or wood siding over a wood frame. Slab construction means a plumbing leak under the foundation often shows up first as a soft spot in flooring or a baseboard that stays damp, rather than an obvious puddle. Older sections of the city may still have galvanized supply lines or cast iron drain lines reaching the end of their service life, and homes built before the 1980s can contain asbestos in older flooring, siding, or insulation that has to be accounted for before demolition begins.
