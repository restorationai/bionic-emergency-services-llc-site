## Restoration emergencies common in Deer Park

Deer Park sits along the Houston Ship Channel in Harris County, and that location drives a lot of what we see. Gulf humidity keeps relative moisture high most of the year, which means any water intrusion, whether from a slow roof leak or a failed supply line, has less chance to dry on its own before secondary damage sets in. Hurricane season brings the heaviest risk: wind-driven rain, downed limbs on roofs, and the kind of heavy regional rainfall that has flooded low-lying parts of Harris County in recent years. Outside of storm season, the more common calls are plumbing failures in older homes, slab leaks pushed up through expansive clay soil, and AC condensation lines that back up in the summer heat and soak carpet pad before anyone notices the smell.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Deer Park

Much of Deer Park's housing stock dates to the mid-century suburban boom, slab-on-grade brick veneer homes built from the 1950s through the 1980s, with newer infill construction mixed in closer to the Ship Channel-adjacent commercial areas. Slab foundations mean a plumbing leak often travels sideways under the slab before it surfaces, so water damage can show up a room away from the actual break. Galvanized and early copper supply lines are common in the older stock and are past their expected service life, which is part of why slab leaks and pinhole failures are a recurring cause of water damage calls.

Harris County's heavy clay soil expands and contracts with moisture swings, putting ongoing stress on slab foundations and the plumbing beneath them. Any structural repair tied to foundation movement or fire rebuild typically requires a permit through the City of Deer Park's building department, and older homes undergoing larger renovation work may need an asbestos or lead-paint assessment depending on the age of the materials involved.
