## Restoration emergencies common in Atascocita

Atascocita sits close to Lake Houston and the West Fork San Jacinto River, and that proximity shapes the kind of damage we see. Heavy rain events back up drainage ditches and retention ponds faster than they can clear, and homes on lower lots or near bayous take on water through grade-level entry points rather than through roof failure. Hurricane season runs June through November, and even storms that stay offshore can dump enough rain locally to push water table levels up and saturate crawlspaces and slab perimeters for days. Between storms, the area's humid subtropical climate keeps indoor humidity high enough that any undetected leak, a slow supply line behind a dishwasher, a slab leak under tile, has a head start on mold growth before anyone notices a smell or a soft spot in the flooring.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Atascocita

Much of Atascocita's housing stock dates from the 1970s through the 2000s, mostly slab-on-grade construction with brick veneer or wood siding over wood-frame walls. Slab foundations mean water intrusion travels laterally under flooring before it becomes visible, so a leak can saturate a much larger footprint than the stain on the carpet suggests.
